How buying raw milk works in England.

Selling raw (unpasteurised) cows’ drinking milk is legal in England, but only directly to the consumer, and only by a registered raw drinking milk producer. In Scotland it is banned.

A registered producer may sell:

  • at the farm gate or in farmhouse catering, including a vending machine on the farm
  • at registered farmers’ markets
  • from a milk round (a vehicle used as a shop)
  • by direct online sales

It is illegal to sell raw milk in any other setting, so you will not find it in a shop or supermarket.

Producers must be registered with the Food Standards Agency, keep a healthy herd free of TB and brucellosis, pass routine inspections and put a health warning on the label.

Before you buy, check that the producer is on the FSA register and read the FSA’s advice on raw drinking milk, including who it says should avoid it.

About the pins

The FSA register gives each producer’s town and postcode area, not a street address, so those pins are approximate. Milk vending machines come from OpenStreetMap volunteers and the milk in them may be pasteurised. Always contact the producer to confirm where and when you can buy.

Check before you travel. Details come from volunteers and public registers and can be out of date. Phone the shop for opening hours and stock.
